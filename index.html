Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Throughout my work in this course, I've learned a lot about the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in the act of making. What taught me these principles the most was my logo design study. I surveyed three different people on their opinions of my logos and their user-friendliness, and received feedback to help revise them. This feedback allowed me to further understand the importance of ethical consideration in design, implementing outside opinions into my work. Creativity is about more than just making something that looks appealing, it's also about user friendliness. This has allowed me to further convey messages through several different mediums, relying on a variety of digital tools.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

In my projects during this class, I used many core design principles to display both creativity and clarity, such as understanding visual hierarchy, color contrasts, and the alignment of elements. A consistent theme in my designs is color consistency, ensuring not only do my designs have balanced layouts, but also coordinated color schemes to enhance viewership. Through these methods, I ensure that my designs not only appear visually appealing, but also function as visual communication

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

I've learned a lot from engaging with others in a workshop environment. During class, we are often asked to discuss with our peers about our experience with our most recent project, providing feedback and critiques. Being able to see such a wide variety of designs and ideas has helped me improve my own work, using this feedback to adjust future designs and further understand what does and doesn't work.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

I've had to revise some of my projects and designs, which I've come to learn is simply a part of the process. The first draft is never a finalized product, instead it serves as starting grounds and an opportunity to further understand what could be improved. It's important to be open to criticism during the process of creating design, since it's rare to get it right the first time.

5. Learn to present your work.

Throughout building my portfolio, I've learned to present my work in a way that appeals to many audiences. At first, I struggled with looking past what I personally thought was appealing, yet later on grew into being able to adapt to an audience. Presenting your work is just as important as creating it, as it showcases the story behind the creation itself.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Throughout these projects, I came to understand design as a rhetorical and ethical act rather than a purely aesthetic one. Each piece required decisions about representation, tone, and accessibility. For example, my “Speech Therapy & Communication Disorders” infographic demanded sensitivity toward how disability and communication are portrayed. Working across digital tools deepened my awareness of how technology utilizes creative expression and influences the persuasive power of design..

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Integrating design principles in my work is what helped me increase the accessibility of my designs and the information it displayed. For example, in my infographic the information used not only determined the research and layout of my design, but also the colors utilized to make it visually appealing. My “Two-Page Spread” project involved reproducing a magazine spread by identifying aesthetics and integrating that into my own work.This challenged me to harmonize typography and image, creating unity between form and content. These exercises revealed how thoughtful application of design principles enhances clarity and rhetorical impact.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Being able to receive feedback from and work with others was crucial to each stage of my designs. These discussions allowed me to implement outside viewpoints into my work and grow from them, increasing accessibility and visual aesthetics along the way. My experience with these discussions emphasized that effective design depends on openness, critical listening, and responsiveness to a variety of perspectives. For my infographic, I focused on the importance of color in the world of branding and what different colors can be used for. I began by designing it with a plain background that contrasted the colorful elements of the information and the topic itself. I received some feedback on what I could add to my infographic design to make it more vibrant. As a result of this feedback, I changed the background color and added some texture to create a more interesting visual.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Revision is an integral part of creation, which requires an open mind across all roles. As a designer, you have to take outside criticism and use it to improve your work, not to be hard on yourself about it. Revising across media strengthened my adaptability and reinforced the importance of process over product. I designed a flyer focusing on how speech and communication therapy can improve accessibility. On the original design, I included information that only described the general importance of speech therapy, not including its relevance to accessibility specifically. After receiving feedback to integrate this information into my flyer, I revised my accessible design flyer by changing the layout and wording of the information to more accurately demonstrate an understanding of how accessible design can be useful. The color scheme, on the other hand, was well received, so I kept that element while revising others.

5. Learn to present your work.

Presentation is the final and most important stage of design. It's what truly shows whether or not your design is successful, and may even lead you back to revision. Presenting my designs strengthened my confidence in identifying design problems, discussing aesthetic choices, and connecting visual outcomes to larger rhetorical and ethical frameworks. While I did not present any of my projects to the class, presenting my work in small groups allowed me to gain vital feedback on what works and what doesn't, such as my advertisement flyer. I created the flyer integrating design elements used by the brand itself to make it feel realistic, using YSL's "Black Opium" perfume and creating a feeling of luxury within font choices and a sparkly background. My group liked everything about the design except for the original plain black background I had initially chosen to use, which led me to changing it into what it is now. Presenting my work has led me to grow more confident in sharing my designs with the world, which further leads to growth through revision and outside views.
